# Premium Editorial direction rationale

## Thesis

Treat a live Client Request as a high-value working publication: the three
representative screens are numbered chapters in one dossier, Request Sources
are preserved as documentary exhibits, and Analysis proposals are editorial
annotations that an Internal User must judge rather than authoritative facts.

The fixed desktop contents rail makes the dossier's structure persistent. On a
narrow viewport it becomes a compact horizontal folio while source exhibits,
field metadata, and marginal notes move into the reading flow without losing
their labels.

## Strongest use cases

- Complex Client Requests where an Internal User must read a sustained record,
  not only clear a queue.
- Review meetings that need a composed narrative alongside dense provenance,
  corrections, exceptions, and workflow status.
- Offer, Contract, and governed Document work where version, authorship, and
  source material should feel deliberate and valuable.

## Deliberate trade-offs

- The direction favors chapter continuity over maximum rows-per-screen. Dense
  registers remain compact, but long source evidence is intentionally allowed
  to occupy reading space.
- AI rationale and provenance begin in collapsed editorial notes so the field
  value and its authority are scannable first. Opening notes is an extra action
  for deep review.
- Large serif hierarchy is reserved for the document's argument and values;
  operational metadata uses a compact sans-serif register to prevent the
  concept from becoming an oversized-headline exercise.

## Risks

- A publication metaphor can make high-frequency operations feel slower than a
  cockpit or queue-led direction.
- Long multilingual source passages may produce very tall chapters, especially
  on narrow screens.
- Collapsed annotations require careful future keyboard, focus, and disclosure
  behavior if this direction is selected and consolidated in T00.
- Premium typographic restraint can drift into excessive whitespace; extension
  should preserve the present rules, dense metadata bands, and explicit next
  actions.

## Visual critique after browser review

At 1440×900, the dark contents rail and running folio make all three screens
read as one working publication rather than unrelated dashboards. The Dashboard
creates a clear lead story, places the blocked Pricing gate in a true margin
note, and brings a compact tabular action register into the first viewport. The
dense register proves the editorial hierarchy can support repeated operational
scanning without turning every record into a spacious feature. The Client
Request balances its large commission title with a dense colophon and documentary
exhibits. That creates deliberate provenance, but the full Request Sources make
the chapter substantially longer than a table-led treatment. The Analysis review
has the strongest operational hierarchy: the red blocked-gate band interrupts
the editorial rhythm appropriately, while symbols plus full state names keep the
four Extracted Field states legible without relying on color.

At 390×844, the contents rail becomes a single horizontal folio, the running head
stays visible, and all three screens remain free of document-level horizontal
overflow. Marginal metadata moves below its related heading, and evidence notes
remain attached to their Extracted Field. Titles still establish the publication
personality, but their narrower mobile scale lets record metadata and the next
section enter sooner. The tabular action register remains horizontally scrollable
inside its own frame rather than widening the document. Long source passages
still require sustained vertical reading. These are deliberate
trade-offs to test with Internal Users, not evidence that this direction should
be selected over the other explorations.

This exploration does not approve the direction, establish production design
tokens, or implement business behavior.
